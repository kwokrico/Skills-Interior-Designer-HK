# Professional practice, appointments and delivery

Load for ethics, consultancy scope/fees, delivery methods, tender documentation, specification strategy and contract administration. Sources: [HKRIDA collection index](../../Reference/HKRIDA-INDEX.md), Professional Guideline (2014), PDF pp. 19–40; Professional Practice volume (2020), chapters 1–6, PDF pp. 4–36. Summaries below are advisory workflow guidance; the signed appointment and applicable law determine actual authority and entitlements. Seek legal review before issuing or interpreting contract wording.

## Ethics and roles

The 2014 code was written for HKIDA designer members. Preserve its context rather than presenting association rules as HK legislation or current membership requirements.

- Disclose financial interests in recommended suppliers and other conflicts before the client makes a decision. Explain the basis of remuneration before accepting an appointment. The historical code also restricts accepting payment from a designer one recommends; check current membership rules for an actual case.
- Protect confidential information, respect copyright, avoid plagiarism and false publicity, credit collaborators and represent others' work fairly. Resolve an existing designer's appointment and handover before taking over an ongoing project. Do not turn the code's historical fee-competition wording into a universal minimum fee or restriction on lawful competitive procurement.
- Identify client PM, consultant designer, contractor PM, QS and specialist consultants separately. Authority to design, instruct, value, certify or approve must be allocated explicitly. Periodic design inspections do not mean continuous site supervision or a guarantee of another party's work.
- Where appointed as certifier, exercise the contractual role impartially; do not promise to change valuations or completion decisions merely because the client requests it. The agent can prepare review material, but cannot issue licensed/statutory sign-off.

## Consultancy appointment checklist

The guideline's agreement is for **client–designer consultancy**, distinct from a **client–contractor fit-out agreement**. Do not substitute HKRG construction payment stages for consultancy fees.

| Topic | Establish in the actual appointment |
|---|---|
| Scope and exclusions (Schedule 1 in the source) | Stage deliverables, revisions, site visits, shop-drawing review, samples/testing, handover and DLP duties; who provides AP/RSE, MEP, QS, licensing, purchasing administration and specialist work |
| Contract documents (Schedule 2) | Complete document list and precedence, revision/date and discrepancy procedure |
| Special conditions (Schedule 3) | Project amendments reviewed together with general terms |
| Other consultants/contractors (Schedule 4) | Named parties, interfaces, design responsibility and client-appointed authority |
| Payment (Schedule 5) | Fee basis, stage evidence, invoice terms, any deposit, interest, taxes where applicable and delayed stages |
| Rates/disbursements (Schedule 6) | Charge-out grades, time records, travel/printing/site staff costs, prior approvals and supporting evidence |
| Change and delay | Who may instruct, written authorisation, changed scope, programme/resource impact, fee assessment and records |
| IP and project use | Copyright ownership, project-use licence, payment conditions, reuse limits, editable-file deliverables, publicity/photo consent |
| Risk and exit | PI insurance and liability wording, suspension/termination grounds, notices, accrued fees, handover and dispute forum |

The source contains specific payment/notice periods, non-refundable-deposit, fee-reduction, liability-cap and arbitration provisions. These are terms of a dated model, **not automatic entitlements, fair defaults or legal advice**. Do not copy them into a live appointment without adaptation and legal review. The source's HKIAC dispute clause is different from HKRG's HKAS reference; identify the actual agreed forum. Publicity rights in a model do not authorize the agent to publish anything.

## Delivery and programme

| Method | Decision implications |
|---|---|
| Separate design consultancy and construction | Client appoints designer and contractor separately; coordinated tender information supports competition and scope comparison. Allocate design, QS and contract-administration duties explicitly. |
| Design and build | One contracted party combines design and execution; establish design responsibility, quality/performance requirements, approval gates, pricing basis and independent checks appropriate to risk. A short programme does not remove approval duties. |
| Hybrid / split packages | Identify package boundaries, responsibility for integration, procurement and testing; gaps cannot be assumed to belong to someone else. |

Use a master programme covering brief, design approvals, authority/landlord interfaces, tender, procurement, construction, commissioning and handover, supported by a detailed construction programme. Mark client decisions, specialist inputs and long-lead release dates. Record the cost, quality and time effects of proposed acceleration rather than simply compressing the drawing stage. Keep a contact/authority matrix and an action/decision register.

## Tender documents and specification strategy

Professional Practice chapters 4–5 expand the existing technical drawing audit. Check instructions to tenderers (deadline, submission rules, programme), form of tender, conditions and appendices, preliminaries, specification, SOR/BQ, tender summary, drawing list/full set and agreed schedules. Audit consistency and qualifications, not simply document presence. Issue clarifications/addenda to all relevant tenderers through a controlled channel.

- **Performance specification:** define the required outcome and verification/acceptance method; allocate specialist design and submittal duties. Do not leave an unmeasurable adjective as the requirement.
- **Prescriptive specification:** state materials, components, workmanship and installation method; coordinate interfaces and identify the exact referenced document/edition.
- **Proprietary specification:** a closed specification identifies a product/system; an open specification permits equivalents. Define equivalence criteria, approval route and evidence so alternatives can be compared before commitment.

The book states that specifications take precedence over drawings. Apply the **actual contract's document precedence**, raise discrepancies and obtain a documented resolution; do not invent a universal ranking. Similarly, historical ArchSD/SFBC examples are references to particular editions, not automatic project requirements.

Distinguish a lump sum for a defined scope from remeasurement, schedule-of-rates and cost-reimbursement arrangements. Define quantities, rates, risk allowances and variation valuation before comparing bids; a lump sum does not mean all future changes are free. Check the actual form for notices, extensions of time, delay damages, valuation/payment evidence, retention, practical completion, defects and final account. Do not import sample percentages, dates or a right to certify into another contract.
