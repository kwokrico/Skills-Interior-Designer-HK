# Roof waterproofing selection

Field source: Renovationeer extract pRUuP0VAGDM; see the [source register](../../../references/source-register.md). Use for coating/system selection, not structural diagnosis or an instruction to access a roof.

## Define exposure before chemistry

| Input | Selection consequence |
|---|---|
| Ownership and access | Confirm roof/common-part responsibility, estate permissions, safe access and working-at-height arrangements |
| Existing assembly | Identify membrane/coating, tiles, insulation, repairs, soundness and compatibility; coating a loose surface does not repair the bond beneath |
| Moisture | Record wet substrate, trapped moisture, possible negative pressure and test method; obtain permitted moisture conditions from the product system |
| Drainage and ponding | Survey falls, outlets, obstructions and overflow paths; distinguish temporary ponding from permanent immersion |
| Movement and details | Cracks, construction/movement joints, parapets, upstands, terminations and penetrations need compatible details |
| Exposure and use | UV, weather, service traffic, occupied terrace use and protection layers affect the required system |
| Installation window | Primer, weather/dew-point restrictions, coverage/thickness, recoat and cure windows, rain protection and test plan |

## Acrylic and PU are starting categories

Compare the **named complete systems**. Acrylic, PU and hybrid products vary in water tolerance, UV exposure, substrate requirements and traffic resistance. Do not declare every PU suitable for standing water or every acrylic unsuitable. A membrane for maintenance access is not automatically a wearing surface for an occupied terrace.

For example, [Sika Vietnam's Sikalastic-590 product page](https://vnm.sika.com/en/construction/roof-systems/cool-roofs/sikalastic-590.html), checked 2026-11-05, describes a PU-acrylic hybrid with limited ponding resistance and UV resistance. This illustrates why chemistry alone is insufficient; it is not a specification for a Hong Kong project. Obtain the current local product/system data and written suitability for the actual exposure.

## Specify and inspect the assembly

- Investigate why the existing system failed: leakage source, substrate movement, debonding, blocked drainage or damaged details. Adding coats can leave those causes untouched.
- Define removal/repair, preparation, primer, membrane and reinforcement, termination details, protection and reinstatement as one coordinated scope. Structural concrete distress follows the AP/RSE route in parent compliance rules.
- Use manufacturer coverage and required film thickness with area and consumption records. Coat count alone is insufficient. Do not transfer film thickness, primer dilution or a five-year recoat interval from a video to another product.
- Agree testing and inspection with the designer/manufacturer and relevant professionals. Do not prescribe roof ponding tests without considering load, drainage, permissions and effects on occupied premises.
- At handover record product/batch, preparation and weather logs, detail photos, test evidence, warranty exclusions, maintenance access and inspection/recoat criteria.
