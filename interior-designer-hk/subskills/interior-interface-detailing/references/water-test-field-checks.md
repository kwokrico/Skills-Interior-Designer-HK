## Windows

- Include water testing explicitly in the window quotation: test extent, responsible contractor, safe access, method, observation period, records, repair and retest. New windows still need verification.
- Observe the complete frame-to-wall perimeter, opening joints, corners and lower sill/reveal corners; check drainage paths. Water can appear away from its entry point. Clear corners alone do not prove the whole assembly sound.
- Observe during application and again afterwards for delayed seepage. The speaker describes about 15 minutes' delay and a 30-minute follow-up; these are field observations, not prescribed durations or acceptance limits.
- Agree a controlled method with the window specialist; protect interiors, electrical equipment, neighbours and common areas. Do not prescribe a car-wash pressure jet, improvised hose connection, leaning outside or exterior work at height. Water pressure can damage seals or create misleading results.
- A hose demonstration cannot certify performance during typhoons or substitute for the applicable window specification/testing standard. Record method, locations, weather, times, photographs and follow-up results; repair and retest failures.

## Bathroom membrane and finished shower

- Confirm the designed wet-area extent, including areas outside the shower where specified, thresholds, upstands and penetrations. Inspect the membrane before covering it.
- Start a flood test only after the named system's required cure and under its approved method and project specification. The video's one-day cure and 48-hour immersion are examples, not universal requirements; a hashtag naming a product does not establish the system or revision used.
- Plan containment, test depth, temporary plugs, overflow prevention, drainage and monitoring. Notify management and arrange access/observation below with the occupier; record adjacent-wall and lower-ceiling conditions before and after testing.
- Mark the initial water level; record start/end time and levels. Consider evaporation, plug leakage and measurement uncertainty before attributing a loss to membrane failure. Water-level stability alone does not prove every interface sound.
- Coloured tracer may improve visibility but needs compatibility, non-staining and disposal approval. No unapproved household dye recipe. A dye trace supports diagnosis; it does not establish liability or the complete leak path.
- After tiling, inspect grout voids, joint consistency, lippage and sealant/movement joints against the agreed specification. Use an approved controlled finished-shower water/drainage test where required; do not instruct an owner to block the drain with a towel and fill without limits.
- Grout is not the waterproofing membrane. A finished-shower leak requires investigation of the membrane, drain, threshold and other interfaces; regrouting alone is not evidence of a lasting repair. Retest and retain the concealed-works record.
