# Material families, standards and emerging products

Source: [Products and Materials volume](../../../../Reference/SS/HKRIDA/application_20200331_3y12O-compressed.pdf), chapters 1–13 (PDF pp. 4–85); see [index and claim limits](../../../../Reference/HKRIDA-INDEX.md). Use this for broader material comparison, standards evidence and novel-product claims; combine with existing countertop, sanitaryware and tile references for those specific products.

## Evidence before specification

Record the actual product, substrate/finish/system, intended use, exposure and required performance. Identify standard number, part, edition, test method, classification and evidence covering the supplied product and installation. A code of practice for installation does not by itself certify a product; a management-system certificate does not establish a board's fire rating. CE marking, a voluntary certification mark and a cited standard have different roles and are not interchangeable approval for HK use.

Do not inherit the book's dated BS/EN editions or product-performance numbers. Obtain the applicable standard and product documents for a project-specific specification. Check whether test evidence covers the complete installed assembly, substrate, adhesive, fixings, thickness and finish.

## Selection questions by family

| Family | Compare and coordinate |
|---|---|
| Stone | Actual mineral/product, porosity, stain/chemical sensitivity, finish and slip evidence for the use; variation, sealing/care, support, joints and installation |
| Timber and wood products | Species/product and moisture movement, grain/veneer matching, substrate and edge treatment, adhesive/emissions, humidity, fixings, sourcing and repairability; hardwood/softwood names alone do not establish durability |
| Glass | Pane type, laminate/interlayer, impact/breakage behaviour, edgework, holes, support and retention after breakage; actual acoustic, UV, solar and fire evidence. Thickness alone does not establish safety |
| Metal | Alloy/grade, finish, corrosion/exposure and dissimilar-material interfaces, fabrication, support, tolerances, touch/cleaning and replaceability. Anodising or a weathering patina is not immunity from damage |
| Plastics / solid surface | Polymer/composite identity, heat/UV response, fire/smoke evidence, movement, scratch/chemical resistance and cleaning; product-approved forming, adhesive, repair and end-of-life route |
| Biocomposites, aerogel, VIP, PCM | Named system and intended application; fire, moisture, durability, thermal evidence, edge/joint/penetration limitations and installation instructions. Do not copy generic insulation multipliers or laboratory properties as installed performance |
| Concrete / UHPC | Weight, support and structural responsibility, curing/shrinkage, joints, finish/porosity and sealing. Do not treat an architectural example as a structural design or assume UHPC always has lower lifecycle impact |
| Textiles, carpets and upholstery | Fibre and construction, traffic/use, abrasion and cleaning method, colourfastness, stains, flame/smoke evidence, backing/underlay/adhesive, emissions and replacement. Acoustic softness is not a tested isolation rating |
| Furniture | User/activity fit, stability, load and mechanism safety, delivery/assembly, maintenance and repair; use ergonomic trials and actual product evidence |

Tempered/toughened and laminated are not mutually exclusive categories: a laminate can use toughened plies. Reject the book's blanket claim that toughened glass is generally unsuitable and its conflation of toughening with chemical treatment. Select the required safety and retention performance with the glazing specialist. Structural glazing/barriers remain subject to the existing AP/RSE halt; this reference supplies no glass/anchor design.

## Emerging-product claims

The book surveys CNC/parametric fabrication, additive manufacturing, biocomposites, nanocoatings, aerogel, VIP, PCM and OLED. Use them as possible avenues for a design problem, not proof of present availability or suitability. Request a prototype/mockup, current technical/safety data, installation limitations, warranty, replacement supply and project-relevant test evidence.

For easy-clean, photocatalytic, antimicrobial, air-purifying or anti-fingerprint surfaces, ask what conditions activate the effect, how wear/cleaning changes it, what was measured and whether by-products/exposure were assessed. Do not infer healthcare infection control from a laboratory surface claim, or substitute a coating for ventilation, moisture control and cleaning. Avoid claims of zero emissions, permanent protection or no maintenance without evidence.

Bio-based, biodegradable, compostable and recyclable describe different properties. Establish the actual composition, processing conditions and available disposal/recovery route; neither a plant-derived feedstock nor a recycled-content label proves a lower whole-life impact. The book's pre-modernism essay is an author's design position, not proof that natural materials always suit users better.

## Verification sources checked 2026-11-06

- [ISO guidance on what conformity to ISO 9001 means](https://www.iso.org/files/live/sites/isoorg/files/archive/pdf/en/iso_9001_supply-chain.pdf): management-system conformity is distinct from product conformity.
- [BSI product-certification guide](https://www.bsigroup.com/globalassets/localfiles/tr-tr/bsi-product-certification-capabilities-tr.pdf): CE conformity marking is not a quality mark; guide is historical, so recheck current market rules for an actual supply decision.
- [Pilkington toughened safety glass](https://www.pilkington.com/en/gbl/architectural-and-technical-glass/product-categories/safety-and-security/pilkington-toughened-glass): describes thermal processing of its toughened product; use the actual manufacturer's system documents for specification.
- [European Commission: bio-based, biodegradable and compostable plastics](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/plastics/bio-based-biodegradable-and-compostable-plastics_en): distinguishes feedstock from end-of-life behaviour.
