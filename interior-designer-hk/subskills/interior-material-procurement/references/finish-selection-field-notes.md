## Tile adhesive

- Most ordinary bags on that job were described as **C1**. **C2** has higher tensile adhesion and is the usual safeguard where the sheet calls for it, including after water, heat ageing, and freeze-thaw. Still specify the EN 12004 class, slip rating, water absorption, rectified edge, format, and movement joints. C2 is not a complete tile spec.
- Dust mixed into the paste is pressed into the bond line, cuts contact area, and pulls site adhesion below the lab number. Indoor work is a reason to use a low-dust bag. Low-dust is less dusty, not dust-free.
- Lay while the surface is open: notched trowel, butter the tile back, straightedge, spacers, levelling clips when tiles vary in thickness. Clean tile back and substrate. Mix as the bag says; scrape the bucket; remix after standing.
- Do not copy a Davco grade name from this extract. The transcript disagrees with itself (TTB V / TTB 5 / TTP5).

## Self-levelling

Use before timber, vinyl, or SPC on an uneven slab. Failure modes if the sheet’s sequence is skipped: bubbles, delamination, waves, poor bond.

| Step the source insists on | Failure if skipped |
|---|---|
| Tap and repair hollow areas first | The leveller leaves with the hollow |
| Clean oil, dust, coatings, loose material | No bond |
| Primer as the sheet says, no leftover film | Film, then debond |
| Water measured first; do not over-water | Separation, or bubbles that cannot be laid |
| Pour from the dead corner; do not over-work; spike-roll; no draught | Waves, separation |
| No early traffic | Footprints; the spoken “walkable the same day” is one product, not a rule |

Thickness, strength, and maximum pour come from the **current datasheet**. Discard the spoken 35 MPa, 7 MPa, primer 1:3, 5–50 mm, and “35MM”. A thick pour over a large area is also a load and a threshold problem — check build-up in `interior-thickness-build-up`.

## Floor and wall by length of stay

| Stay | Field approach |
|---|---|
| About 3–5 years | Cover rather than strip. SPC is a short-stay floor; it wears and is usually left behind. Do not use it to bury a failed membrane or a fall to a floor waste |
| About 10–20 years | Spend on the base. Tile is easier to keep |

- SPC and seamless timber (Quick-Step type in the source) swell when wet. Sheet vinyl was the cheap rental floor because it lifts. Timber is harder to keep in an ordinary small flat; the source does not recommend it unless the look is the point.
- Microcement rarely pays in a small HK flat. 文化石 chips easily — experience, not a test. Ask whether a material can form the drawn bullnose.
- Do not finish what nobody sees and what cabinet install will scratch: tiles behind kitchen cabinets, walls fully hidden by tall units. Where a strip of wall remains, a panel can be more practical than a perfect plaster face.
- A storage platform that will leave in a few years: screed only underneath, not tile. **Wet areas are not this rule.** Do not skip waterproofing because a cabinet will cover the wall.
- Hob zone: non-combustible stainless sheet. Do not record a fire-board rating the speaker then retracted.
- Tile set-out: state the bond (亂工、正工、人字鋪), which tile, and where tile one starts. Put **full tiles on the seen edge** so a run of cuts (老鼠尾) is not what you look at. Site can be out by enough to create that run; the quote-stage plan will not show it.
- HK$/ft² figures in the transcripts (tile, screed, SPC, a call-out for a short wall) are talk, not a rate book. Use `hk-brand-unit-prices.md` or a live quote.
