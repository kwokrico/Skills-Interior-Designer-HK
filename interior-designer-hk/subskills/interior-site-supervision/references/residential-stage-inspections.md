# Residential stage inspections

## Demolition release

- Walk the agreed demolition plan item by item, including recesses and concealed/overlooked positions, before the next trade starts. A missed item is not authority to remove an unapproved structural or fire element. Record unresolved scope and safety hazards; isolate unsafe areas and apply existing compliance halts.
- Reconcile retained items with the signed retention list. Protect fixed sills, fans, AC and other retained fittings before work; arrange secure off-site storage of removable items where agreed, including who disconnects, transports and reinstalls them.
- Photograph the common route from lobby through lift and corridor to the flat before works/deliveries, then compare after demolition. Date and locate images; agree condition records with management where possible and report new damage promptly. A post-demolition photograph alone cannot prove a defect predated the works or establish liability.

## Services before concealment

- Arrange the hold point after pipes/routes and wiring are installed but before render, tile or ceiling closure. Review against the coordinated point/route drawings with the responsible trades; keep necessary valves, junctions and maintenance access available.
- Distinguish conduits exposed inside a false-ceiling void from conduits chased into the original slab/ceiling. “Whole flat concealed wiring” needs a scope drawing and exclusions; it does not automatically include chasing the structural ceiling. Do not authorize chasing beams/slabs on the basis of a price agreement.
- The video says wires must enter conduit. Use the actual compliant wiring system instead of converting this into a rule that every cable type uses the same enclosure. A registered electrical contractor must confirm protection, support, routing and verification against applicable EMSD requirements. Hidden wiring is not exempt from protection.
- Arrange required water-pressure testing before closure; reconcile the quotation and inspection plan without treating omission from a quote as permission to skip required tests. Record the pipe/system, tested extent, approved method, pressure/duration, gauge, observations, witness and retest outcome. Do not invent test pressure from the video. Electrical inspection/testing and completion evidence remain separate requirements.
- Take wide and close photographs of each service route, with room/wall ID, date, orientation, reference dimensions and a marked drawing. Include crossings and changes; update after rectification and retain in as-builts/O&M. Photographs aid later planning but are not clearance to drill: verify current services/conditions with competent detection and safe working methods before fixing.

Official boundary check: [EMSD sample electrical test checklist](https://bestpractice.emsd.gov.hk/en/electrical-installations/useful-forms), checked 2026-11-07, includes conduit/trunking or metallic-sheath protection for wiring inside false ceilings. Confirm the applicable current [Wiring Regulations CoP](https://www.emsd.gov.hk/en/electricity_safety/publications/codes_of_practice/index.html) and actual cable system with the registered contractor rather than prescribing from a transcript.

## Paint interim and completion gates

- Agree the scope of 剷底: removal of existing coatings is not automatically stripping paper-thin skim/plaster to concrete. Inspect the exposed base after stripping for loose/hollow material, damp and defects; record necessary remedial scope before covering it. Do not scrape structural concrete or hide an unresolved leak under primer.
- Before emulsion/topcoat, inspect filler/plaster repair, plane/straightness, pinholes, sanding and material junctions. Correct defects at this stage; use an agreed sample and practical viewing conditions rather than disrupting each application pass.
- Confirm compatible sealer/primer, filler, undercoat and topcoat from the named system, with substrate moisture/cure and recoat conditions. The video's sequence and “two coats before joinery, one after” are programme examples, not a universal coat count or waterproofing specification. Agree final-coat timing and protection after joinery damage/finishing-joint repairs.
- Specify corner beads/protection at vulnerable exposed corners where appropriate, including substrate compatibility and price; they cannot guarantee every existing corner becomes square. Define the joinery/paint finishing boundary.
- At completion, inspect uniform colour, obvious brush marks, pinholes, scratches and repaired joints at agreed distances/lighting. DECO-TV's daylight and at-least-one-metre example broadly overlaps HKRG paint inspection; record any project-specific requirement for artificial/grazing light in advance.
- Log all cracks. Do not automatically classify one as harmless because weather changed. Minor confirmed drying/movement touch-ups may be scheduled by agreement within DLP/notice periods; debonding, blistering, active damp, progressive or suspected structural cracks need prompt investigation. Do not wait an unspecified “climate cycle” before reporting defects.

Use [site instruction](../../../references/templates/site-instruction.md) for directed rectification and [snag log](../../../references/templates/snag-defect-log.md) for follow-up. Visual owner review does not certify concealed or regulated work.

## Demolition setup and immediate follow-up — batch 3

Sources: EwgwJ_1EikI, FQvYykYDKwc, raODhYRE8Qo, O8Kfn-lAFyI; [coverage and archives](../../../../Reference/DECO-TV-BATCH3-COVERAGE-2026-11-07.md).

- Before vibration/demolition, assess retained heavy pendant lights and vulnerable fittings for competent removal/storage; arrange safe temporary lighting before losing the existing lights. Identify service isolation with the responsible trades. Secure trailing cables and keep tools/materials out of circulation/escape routes; use suitable secured entrance dust control, not an assumed safe wet towel. Confirm management forms, protection, scaffold and shutdown arrangements against actual lead times rather than adopting “3–7 days”.
- After demolition and before releasing the next trade, check leaking supply points, exposed electrical hazards, common-route cleanliness and reported new leaks/cracks/spalling in neighbouring premises. Make unsafe conditions safe through the responsible trades, document/report and close outstanding hazards; do not assume causation or liability from timing alone.
- Check whether removing sanitary fittings also removed the effective trap/water seal. Have the plumbing trade identify and suitably temporarily cap unused open drainage connections; retain necessary drainage/venting functions and record reinstatement/testing before use. A label “U-trap” alone is not proof the remaining arrangement works.
- Rust-associated cracking, loose concrete or suspected reinforcement corrosion invokes the existing structural safety halt: isolate the affected area and refer for competent professional assessment/AP/RSE as appropriate. The O8Kfn-lAFyI chisel/coat/patch demonstration is not an approved repair design or permission to expose reinforcement and cover it cosmetically.
