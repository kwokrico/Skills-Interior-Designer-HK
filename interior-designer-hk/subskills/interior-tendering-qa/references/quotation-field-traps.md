# Quotation field traps

> Source: 工程師裝修學堂 transcripts extracted 2026-11-01 (`Reference/renovationeer_extract`). Field commentary, not HKEDCA, HKRG, or statute.
> On conflict: statute, AP/RSE, manufacturer method statement, then the signed contract.
> Do not copy spoken numbers the transcript flags as Whisper errors.

When the contract adopts HKRG, payment stages in `hkrg-standard-quotation-contract.md` still govern. Use this file to compare bids and to classify variations before those clauses are filled.

## Price the locked layout

- A quote-stage plan is not the design. Lock furniture layout, then price **by item**.
- A package price (套餐價 / 一口價) is a lump sum. An omitted item can be treated as given up. Stretching a stated cabinet length has no rate basis unless the contract says so.
- Rates with no quantities are not comparable. A Customs case (明思設計) is cited in the source as a quote that moved from about HK$200,000 to about HK$700,000 because only rates were fixed. Treat that as a **warning**, not as a legal definition of fraud.
- Re-measurement lets additions and omissions use the same rates. Domestic fit-out has no standard bill; three headline totals are not the same job.

## Compare on content, not the total

| Check | What to do |
|---|---|
| Missing lines | If bidder A priced a necessary item and bidder B left it blank or “to be confirmed”, get B’s price before calling the totals equal |
| Latent re-price | Mark lines that say 「之後先報」 or that will be remeasured after opening up |
| Material grade | Low / mid / high; who buys (owner vs contractor); import vs unbranded |
| Optional items | Keep optionals **out** of the compared total. A low core plus a sky-high optional still wins if scoring ignores the optional, and the owner cannot re-tender once site has started |
| Design differences | Score light troughs, curves, and special shapes yourself. Do not hide them inside the lump sum |

Separate the design fee from the works. Tiles, sanitaryware, doors, locks, appliances, glass, steel, and special joinery shapes are often 「另計物料」. Ask for an experience budget of everything still to be spent, and whether any design fee is credited to the works.

## Variations

| Kind | Who carries it |
|---|---|
| Foreseeable omission inside the priced scope | Contractor, unless the lump sum expressly gave the item up |
| Hidden works, visible only after opening up | Provisional sum or owner contingency. **Not automatic** under a lump sum — follow the signed contract |
| Necessary for completion (safety, a leak, drainage that will not work) | True variation basis |
| “Want” items | Optional. Do not treat them as a completion variation |
| Same trade, still on site | Contract rates, if the contract says so |
| Trade already demobilised (call the electrician back to move one socket) | Change of sequence. The original rate need not apply |

More work moves the completion date. Use the programme as the baseline. Fairest arithmetic, when the contract allows it, is add and omit at contract rates. Ask the firm to build the extra from those rates. A few hundred dollars of goods can cost thousands to install; planning and labour are not 「順手」.

## Field observations, not standard clauses

- Deposits in the source run about 16% to 30%. That is what some firms ask, not an HKRG stage.
- Keep a contingency line **separate** from the contract sum. No percentage is given in the source. Who pays unforeseen opening-up follows the contract, not a contractor default that the owner always pays.

## Do not copy from the transcripts

- The phrase 「Session Subject to Exist」 (a garbled “provisional / subject to”).
- “Every rate-only quote is fraud.”
- “Unforeseen spalling is always the owner’s cost,” or “a short-stay owner may leave rusted bars.” Spalling repair is a statutory item — see `interior-statutory-compliance` / `structural-and-hos-alerts.md`.
- A design-fee credit figure as market practice. Ask; do not assume it.
