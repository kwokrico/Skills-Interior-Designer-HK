# Contractor due diligence and payment chain

Field sources: Rocky extracts Nx0Vxygbb4U and yH5CvYd4awc, supported by I2q_KSbzomI. See the [source register](../../../references/source-register.md). These are procurement checks, not a guarantee of solvency or legal advice.

## Match the service to the brief

| Route | Clarify before appointment |
|---|---|
| Direct trades / foreman | Who designs, draws, measures, coordinates interfaces and inspects? Who is the contracting party for each trade? |
| Works contractor | Which design/documentation services are included; who coordinates owner-supplied joinery and appliances? |
| Design-and-build firm | Named designer and site lead; design deliverables, changes, shop drawings, subcontractors and single-point responsibilities |
| Joinery supplier | Whether demolition, plumbing, electrical and making good are included or separately contracted |
| Referral platform | Whether it only introduces firms or is a contractual party; actual scope of inspection, payment or dispute services |

A platform introduction, showroom, professional title or registration is evidence to investigate, not assurance of workmanship or completion. Do not rank businesses by their addresses or appearance alone.

## Evidence checklist

| Check | Evidence to request | Record the unresolved risk |
|---|---|---|
| Contract identity | Legal entity/name and applicable company/business registration details; authorised signatory; contact/address | Trading name differs from the party liable for delivery |
| Relevant capability | Completed comparable work, reference contacts with consent, proposed team and site lead | Renderings or personal credentials do not prove the firm delivered that scope |
| Regulated work | Current registration, permitted work scope and named responsible contractor/worker where applicable | A general business registration is not an electrical, gas or building-work authorisation |
| Insurance | Project period, insured activities and parties, limits/exclusions and subcontractor arrangements | Insurance exists but excludes the actual work, such as scaffolding |
| Documentation | Scope, dimensions, quantities/rates, material grades, drawings, exclusions, programme, variations, acceptance, DLP and dispute terms | Incomplete quote or unresolved design creates incomparable totals |
| Design-stage fees | What quotation/layout/detailing costs, what is delivered, when payments are due, any credit/refund terms | Verbal promise that a deposit buys all future drawings |

Check current official registers when verifying a particular firm or regulated scope; retain the checked name, scope, source and date. Do not label a firm fraudulent from missing information alone. Follow [quotation-field-traps.md](quotation-field-traps.md) for price comparison and the adopted HKRG/HKEDCA contract for payment and variations.

## Intermediaries and factory orders

Map **owner → contracting entity → intermediary → manufacturer → installer**. Identify which parties are actually in the contract, who receives money and issues receipts, who authorises production, and who owes delivery, installation and warranty service. A messaging group with the factory does not establish that a deposit reached it.

Before the relevant payment milestone, obtain the agreed evidence: signed scope and approved shop drawing revision, order acknowledgement identifying the job, production/delivery status and receipt from the contractual payee. Verify a changed payee/account through an established contact. Do not invent a universal deposit percentage or advise arbitrary payment withholding; proposed safeguards must fit the agreed contract and receive legal review where needed.

If evidence conflicts, seek clarification before the affected appointment or order. For an existing stalled job, preserve contract, drawings, approvals, receipts and dated communications; use the contract's notice/dispute route and professional advice rather than promising recovery or issuing legal demands as the designer.
