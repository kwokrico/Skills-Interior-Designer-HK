## Spalling (士波令)

- Spalling is concrete broken off with rusting steel. A burst soffit is not automatically a dangerous building, and it is not something to watch for a few more years when the area is large.
- Concrete takes compression; steel takes tension. Cover concrete is usually **not** counted in the strength calculation. Cover falling off is not the same as section loss of the bar. Rust travels along the bar.
- Rust on the original main structure matters more than rust on infill between beams or columns. Cantilever and balcony locations are stricter than ordinary infill.
- Repair is a Buildings Ordinance submission, not a private skim or a coat of paint. Ordinary slabs and beams may or may not fall under a minor-works class — the AP checks the current schedule item by item. Do not assume Class I.
- The designer does not apply a “10–20% section loss” eye test and does not specify mortar strength. Sequence awareness only, for coordination: remove loose concrete, derust, protect the bar, bond coat, repair mortar compatible with the parent concrete. Product grades in the transcripts are not a specification.

## What a test can and cannot say

「冇明顯危險」 means no immediate instability on the day of inspection. It is not “complies with the drawings”. An occupation permit does not prove as-built reinforcement.

| Method | It can | It cannot |
|---|---|---|
| Rebound hammer | Indicate surface hardness | Prove bar size, position, or strength of the member |
| Cover meter | Indicate cover thickness | Count bars or prove diameter |
| GPR | Indicate bar position and spacing | Replace opening-up |
| Core compression | Test that sample | Speak for every bay |
| Opening-up | Count, diameter, cover at that location | Be skipped because an OP exists |

Use several methods, then compare with the approved drawing. Reported shortfalls on one house (One Stanley, bars in places about 41% to 75% below the drawings) are **that case**, not a slab rule. A local slab defect is a different problem from a defect in a main beam, column, or the whole load path. Do not turn “bars not as drawn” into “the block must be rebuilt”.

## Balcony glass barrier

Load path: person → glass → shoe → fixing → **structural slab**. The base is the fulcrum. An anchor in tile or screed is not an anchor in the structural concrete. Reject two expansion anchors set into the floor finish.

- Open the finish along the base down to the structural slab. Build the barrier first. Waterproof, make good, and tile afterwards.
- After the anchor reaches the structural face, do a pull-out test. Weak concrete can burst the hole as the anchor is tightened; that fixing then does little.
- Glass: heat-soak toughened (熱浸強化玻璃), with the certificate. Standing on the barrier on the day it was fixed is not the test.
- Protective barriers are often discussed as minor works. Complex, sensitive, life-safety, or cantilever cases may need an A&A submission. “Class I” is a question for the AP, not a self-classification.
- A stated lever of about 1.1 m is a teaching picture. Barriers are designed to the code line load, not to that sketch.

## HOS and MIC

- Modular Integrated Construction (MIC, 組裝合成) modules are boxes. The four sides are often structural. Do not demolish them. Housing Authority drawings can require 釘契 or an order to reinstate. Temporary module walls are included: do not remove them either.
- “Non-structural” does not mean exempt, and it does not waive HA consent, fire compartmentation, stacks, or concealed services. The owner does not “handle fire afterwards”.
- Wall thickness is a **case**, not every block. 麗翠苑 (about 280 ft², intake 2019, conventional) had kitchen and toilet walls about 85 mm. 安柏苑 (about 280 ft², intake 2025, MIC) had a kitchen wall about 435 mm and another about 400 mm, consistent with two module walls of about 200 mm back to back. Confirm on the approved drawings for that flat. Furniture can move; the lost area does not come back.
- Every module joint is a construction joint. Workmanship that reverses a waste fall, silts a pipe, or splits a joint is the fit-out risk, including toilets and kitchens that leaked during later fitting-out on one estate. A spoken tolerance of 10–20 mm is anecdotal. Do not specify it.
- Factory completion claims (share of work off site, programme, waste) in the source are anecdotal. Do not write them as performance.
