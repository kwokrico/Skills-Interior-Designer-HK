# Premises feasibility, licensing and adaptive reuse

Source: [Interior Construction, Codes and Regulations volume](../../../../Reference/SS/HKRIDA/application_20200331_8lvVS_compressed.pdf), chapters 1–9, PDF pp. 4–71. Educational framework; do not use its historical code tables, minor-works counts, notice periods or licensing examples as current requirements.

## Separate the approval layers

Check approved existing plans/use, land lease and planning constraints, proposed building works, fire strategy, accessibility, services, occupation/use and any operating licence. Landlord/estate consent, BD building-work procedures and an operating licence address different matters. An attractive layout or a former tenant's licence is not evidence of current feasibility.

Classify the actual proposed works with the appropriate competent professional: exempted work, prescribed minor works or work needing prior approval/consent. Do not infer a class from a photo, textbook item number or the word 'interior'. Escalate structural work, additional floors/mezzanines, void infill, external barriers, enclosure of service areas or changes affecting escape and protected lobbies before design commitment. Existing hard stops remain in force.

The Communication volume PDF p. 5 broadly describes AP-signed construction documents. This does **not** mean every interior drawing requires AP approval, nor that the agent can supply an AP signature. Determine the work-specific statutory route first. Conversely, exemption from a particular submission does not waive safety, lease or other applicable requirements.

## Special occupancies

| Project | Early feasibility and coordination questions |
|---|---|
| Restaurant / food premises | Confirm intended food operation and licence category, permitted use and approved condition before committing to a site. Coordinate food-preparation/service flows, hygienic cleanable surfaces, ventilation/exhaust and discharge route, drainage, sanitation, fire/escape and accessibility with the licensing team. Identify FEHD, BD and fire requirements separately and use current application guidance. |
| Residential care home | Establish resident needs and care model, operator/licence category and the applicable SWD code. Coordinate assisted movement/evacuation, staff observation, privacy/dignity, sanitary assistance, non-slip/legible routes, sensory cues and service access. A care-home case study is not a generic compliant room layout. |
| Adaptive reuse / heritage hospitality | Survey existing fabric and approved use; identify heritage constraints, conservation significance and responsible authorities/professionals. Test the new use against structure, fire, accessible routes, building services and licensing. Prefer compatible/reversible interventions where feasible and document any approved alternative approach; heritage status does not itself grant a compliance exemption. |

## Fire and inclusive-use interfaces

Distinguish fire resistance (including the relevant loadbearing/integrity/insulation criteria) from reaction-to-fire surface behaviour. Fire doors, glazing, frames, hardware, seals, installation and penetrations form an assembly; a tested pane or leaf alone cannot establish the installed rating. Chain to fire-life-safety for protected routes, lobbies, smoke control, shutters and sprinkler interfaces.

Plan accessibility as a continuous journey: arrival, entrance, routes/doors, level changes, controls, sanitary facilities and information. Tactile guidance, visual contrast, audible information, rest points and assistance needs must suit the actual users and current requirements. Pair with ergonomics for universal-use trials, and environmental graphics for wayfinding.
