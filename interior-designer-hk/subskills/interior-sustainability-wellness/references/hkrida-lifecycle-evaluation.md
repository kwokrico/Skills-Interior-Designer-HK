# Lifecycle design and environmental evaluation

Sources: [Human Environment Needs](../../../../Reference/SS/HKRIDA/application_20200331_Sc4da.pdf), chapter 5; [Construction, Codes and Regulations](../../../../Reference/SS/HKRIDA/application_20200331_8lvVS_compressed.pdf), chapters 5–6; Products and Materials chapters 7, 11–13. See [collection index](../../../../Reference/HKRIDA-INDEX.md). Retain the methods below; the books' historical scheme names/credits, thresholds and case-study savings are not current certification criteria.

## Design for repeated use

Evaluate retaining existing fabric and furniture before replacement. Consider adaptable layouts, reusable partitions, modular components, accessible service zones, reversible connections, future disassembly and replaceable wear parts. Check that reuse still meets condition, fire, hygiene and functional needs. Design out unnecessary layers and waste; plan fabrication, offcuts, packaging, site sorting and actual recovery routes.

Assess material extraction/manufacture, transport, installation, maintenance, operational use, repair/replacement and end of life. A material's recycled content, renewable feedstock or local origin alone cannot establish the best lifecycle result. Balance durability and maintenance with embodied impacts, operating energy/water, user comfort and the expected refurbishment cycle.

## Use the right evaluation

| Method | Question and evidence |
|---|---|
| LCA: life-cycle assessment | What environmental impacts occur across the defined life cycle? Define goal/scope, functional unit and boundary; inventory inputs/outputs, assess impacts and interpret uncertainty. Compare products delivering the same function over the same service period and compatible datasets/boundaries. |
| LCEA: life-cycle energy assessment | How much energy is embodied and consumed across the stated life cycle? State what manufacturing, replacement, operational and end-of-life energy is included; do not present energy alone as total environmental impact. |
| LCC: life-cycle costing | What does the option cost over the agreed period? Include purchase/installation, operation, maintenance, replacements and end-of-life costs; state period, price basis and financial assumptions. Lowest first cost need not be lowest LCC. |
| POE: post-occupancy evaluation | Does the occupied environment meet the brief? Use user feedback, observation and relevant measurements; capture occupancy and operating conditions, actions and follow-up. Keep satisfaction, measured performance and certification distinct. |

Combine methods when appropriate, showing tradeoffs rather than converting them into an unsupported single 'green' claim. Obtain product-specific evidence for aerogel, VIP, PCM and nanomaterial options; do not extrapolate textbook performance factors or assume advanced materials always lower carbon.

## Comfort and certification

Coordinate source control, ventilation and moisture, daylight/glare, acoustics, thermal conditions and user controls. Low VOC content and tested emissions are different evidence; a low-emitting product does not establish whole-room IAQ. Document construction dust/noise/air controls and pre-occupation cleaning/commissioning appropriate to the actual system.

For BEAM Plus, LEED, WELL or another scheme, identify the exact current tool/version, project boundary, eligible credits, responsible assessor, evidence and verification process. Separate mandatory legislation from voluntary ratings and client goals. Never copy the book's old credit tables or case-study distances/percentages as live requirements or promise certification from a narrative checklist.
