# Residential FF&E fit, selection and compact-flat planning

Field sources: Rocky extracts 3j3dmdMefwA, lIRrCq80WCU and w8M6m2ixC00. See the [source register](../../../references/source-register.md). Use for design coordination and user trials; do not turn comfort or promotional claims into medical advice.

## Beds and mattresses

Record the user's height, mobility, transfer needs, sleeping preferences and partner requirements. Trial the actual mattress/support combination where possible. Firmness is the initial feel; support concerns how the combination accommodates the person. More thickness, more zones or pocket springs do not by themselves establish better sleep or treatment of pain.

| Check | Design action |
|---|---|
| Width and length | Use actual metric product dimensions; nominal foot sizes vary. Allow for frame, headboard, bedding and movement around the bed |
| Finished bed height | Add floor/platform, frame/support and mattress; compare with the user's seated/transfer trial, including mattress compression |
| Storage bed | Check lifting mechanism, mattress weight limits, opening envelope, wall/headboard clearance and safe operation |
| Mattress construction | Ask about support and comfort layers, partner disturbance, ventilation, care and warranty conditions; verify rather than rank by material alone |
| Delivery | Check packaged dimensions, permitted bending, weight, lift/stair route, doorway and turns before ordering |

A thick mattress on a high storage base can make access difficult even if each product is suitable alone. Adjust the base or product selection; do not automatically add a step or recommend a lower bed for every older person. Test transfer and trip risks with the actual user. Verify any allergy, health or exclusive-certification claim against its scope; do not promise clinical outcomes.

## Sofas and recliners

Specify overall and usable seat width separately from the advertised number of seats. Trial seat height/depth, lumbar support, back angle, arms and headrest with the intended users; a single preferred dimension is not a population rule.

- Identify upholstery by component: contact surfaces, sides and back. Ask for the actual material specification and care limits. Full/partial leather, grain descriptions, coated leather, bonded material, PU/PVC and textiles should not be collapsed into one hierarchy inferred from showroom speech.
- For “cat-scratch” or performance fabric, request available test evidence and warranty exclusions; do not guarantee that claws cannot damage it. Include cleaning, removable covers, replacement availability and sample appearance under project lighting.
- Draw the full recline/sofa-bed envelope, wall offset, cable and power access, circulation and service route. “Zero wall” does not mean no front clearance or maintenance space.
- Check pinch/crush zones, controls, stability and manufacturer loading/use restrictions, especially with children or pets. Do not prescribe aftermarket wheels or supports to cure a tipping product; refer it to the supplier.

## Compact-flat planning

For a central cluster of doors (五門歸心), map door swings and required circulation before placing a sofa, table or TV. Overlap compatible circulation zones rather than reserving duplicate corridors, but retain safe usable routes while chairs, appliances and doors are operating.

For opposing bedroom entries (眼鏡房), compare a shared entry/recess, furniture relocation and door-type options. Assess the room area lost against the useful wall gained, privacy, acoustics, lighting and access. A cabinet divider does not replace a statutory/fire/acoustic partition.

Slim basins, split-fold/slide-fold doors, narrower sofa arms or loose furniture may solve a constraint without moving a wall. Link door/hardware options to millwork and appliance movement to MEP. Kitchen doors, open kitchens, HOS/MIC walls and structural alterations must follow existing statutory/fire routes; the layout example is not permission to alter them.
