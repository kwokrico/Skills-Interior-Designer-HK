# Lighting schedule review — evidence outstanding

**500 lux initially does not prove the maintained target or comfortable screen work. Hold performance acceptance pending the evidence below.** Confirm the project location, applicable design standard/edition, contractual maintained target, task areas and assessment plane before judging compliance; 500 lux is not automatically a universal statutory requirement.

The maintenance relationship is:

**Maintained illuminance = initial illuminance × overall maintenance factor (MF).**

MF accounts for output depreciation and dirt on luminaires and room surfaces over a stated period. The supplier must support the factor and maintenance programme; it cannot be assumed to equal 1. If the required maintained target is 500 lux, an initial 500 lux has no allowance for depreciation. Define all losses and count each once: do not multiply an overall MF by another overlapping light-loss factor (LLF). [CIE maintenance-factor definition](https://cie.co.at/eilv/753).

| Screen reflections and luminance | Check visible luminaires and windows reflected in screens, screen orientation/tilt, luminaire luminance/distribution and brightness contrasts in the field of view. Lux measures light arriving at a surface; it does not establish the luminance seen by the user or absence of reflected glare. |
| Daylight and controls | Assess daytime and electric-light-only conditions, window glare and shading, task-light adjustment, dimming scenes and daylight-control settings. | Commissioning and user verification | Provide an agreed measurement protocol and calibrated-meter results on the stated task grid, recording daylight, blinds, controls and actual installed fixtures. Verify glare/reflections from occupied seated positions and undertake a representative workstation mock-up or user review. Initial measurements validate initial performance; maintained performance also depends on the substantiated depreciation and maintenance basis. |

Return the schedule for a coordinated calculation report, completed product submittals, maintenance basis and commissioning plan. Release the performance claim only when these demonstrate the agreed maintained-lighting and visual-comfort criteria. Increasing output alone is not a sufficient remedy and may worsen glare.
