Key delivery is evidence of possession, not by itself proof of completion, acceptance or waiver of defects. The following items need separate treatment:

| Item | Classification and action | Evidence needed to close |

| Bunk bed cannot be accessed | Functional failure, potentially a safety issue—not merely a cosmetic snag. Do not use the inaccessible bunk or improvise climbing access. | Missing cabinet panel | Incomplete scope. It may be a minor outstanding item only if purely decorative and function/safety and the contract allow this. If it provides support, guarding or enclosure, treat its absence as a functional/safety issue. | Identify panel location, part ID, dimensions, finish and function; contractor/supplier confirms supply and installation dates; inspect fit, fixings, finish and cabinet operation after installation. |
| Keys handed over | Record the actual handover date and any agreed limitations. Do not record unrestricted completion while significant functional defects remain unresolved. | Signed outstanding-work schedule and, if appropriate, a specifically agreed partial-handover record. |

For the missing panel, request a confirmed factory delivery and installation date; a video's two-to-three-week anecdote is not a supplier commitment. Any partial handover should identify usable areas, excluded items, return-work access, protection, responsible parties, deadlines, reinspection and payment effects.
