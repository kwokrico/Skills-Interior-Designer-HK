# Bedroom curtain/blind brief — provisional, for survey and supplier development

**Opening:** inward-opening bedroom window beside wardrobe. **Required:** daytime privacy, night blackout and motor control. **Status:** products, reveal measurements and mounting arrangement unconfirmed; no fabrication dimensions or electrical connection locations released.

A two-layer arrangement is the design intent: independently operated light-filtering privacy layer and blackout layer. Final choice between curtains, blinds or a combination depends on the measured window and wardrobe operating envelopes. The missing product and survey data hold final specification and ordering, but do not prevent this performance brief.

| Brief item | Requirement / coordination action |
|---|---|
| Daytime privacy | Filter daylight while screening views from the actual neighbouring/public sightlines. Agree how much outward view is required. Test a sufficiently large sample at the window; privacy varies with lighting contrast and fabric openness. Do not assume a sheer gives night privacy. |
| Night blackout | Use an opaque layer with coordinated head, side, centre and bottom light control. Blackout fabric alone does not make a blackout room. Agree acceptable residual light with the client and demonstrate the proposed assembly under bright exterior conditions. |
| Window operation | Record full inward sash swing and handle projection. Keep brackets, tracks, fabric, side channels and raised blind cassette/stack outside the required operating envelope. Confirm whether window opening with either layer deployed is required; show the intended operating sequence. |
| Wardrobe interface | Record wardrobe face, doors, handles and full door/drawer opening. Show curtain stack-back and fabric depth, or blind mechanism projection, in relation to them. Neither stored nor moving treatment should obstruct wardrobe use, window handles or access for cleaning. |
| Motor control | Independently control privacy and blackout layers; provide accessible local open/stop/close controls, with bedside control if desired. App/automation integration is optional and must be confirmed for the selected system. Agree noise expectations, preset positions and power-failure behaviour. |
| Power and access | Supplier to identify motor side, supply type, cable route, connector/driver and servicing envelope. Coordinate power with the electrical designer/installer before concealment. If battery operation is selected, demonstrate safe charging/replacement access. Keep motor and connections accessible without dismantling the wardrobe. |
| Material and upkeep | Approve colour, texture, lining, fullness/pleat or shade construction and external-facing appearance by sample. Obtain cleaning, removal, shrinkage and replacement guidance. Check condensation exposure and avoid obstructing ventilation openings. Obtain applicable child-safety and installation documentation for the selected system, including any backup cords. |

**Options to develop after survey**

- **Separate privacy and blackout curtains:** assess wall/ceiling mounting and parking space beyond the glazing. A one-sided stack away from the wardrobe may help, but only if its full stack and motor fit. Coordinate centre overlap, side returns and head light control.
- **Privacy blind plus blackout curtain:** useful to test if the curtain stack can be accommodated and the blind cassette clears the inward-opening sash when raised.
- **Two blind layers:** investigate where lateral curtain parking is unavailable. Confirm the full raised rolls/stacks, brackets and any blackout side channels clear the sash and handles. Reveal mounting is not approved simply because the fabric fits within the opening.

**Required survey and supplier submission before order**

1. Measure opening width/height at multiple positions, reveal depth, head/sill levels, squareness, wall/ceiling mounting substrate, sash hinges and full swing, handle projection, adjacent wardrobe and nearby lights/vents. Record photographs and dimensions from consistent finished-surface datums.
2. Submit a coordinated plan, elevation and section: planning at 1:50, critical head/jamb/wardrobe details at 1:5. Show both layers deployed and parked, sash and wardrobe fully open, motor access and light-leak paths.
3. Attach actual product drawings showing cassette/track depth, maximum raised stack or roll, curtain stack-back, brackets, tolerances, fixing requirements, motor limits and service access. All dimensions remain **TBC** until this evidence and site measurements agree.
4. Client/designer to approve privacy and blackout samples/mock-up, controls, maintenance access, budget and lead time. Supplier and installer to resolve dimensional clashes before manufacture.

At handover, demonstrate every window and wardrobe opening, each motor independently, stop/preset functions and agreed power-failure procedure. Check daytime privacy and blackout against the approved mock-up; provide care instructions, control setup and warranties.

Project jurisdiction, any required escape-window function and estate restrictions remain to be confirmed before installation. Preserve required ventilation and escape/access functions; refer any conflict for the appropriate statutory/fire review.

**Basis:** the skill’s furnishings/window-treatment reference, drawing on Ching and Binggeli, *Interior Design Illustrated*, 3rd edition, pp. 346–349, supplies the design checklist. It is not a product specification; actual manufacturer documentation governs dimensions and installation.
