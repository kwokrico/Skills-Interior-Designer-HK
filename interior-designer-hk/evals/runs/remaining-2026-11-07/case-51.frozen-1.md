# Early concept comparison — 6 m × 3 m living/dining room

**Brief:** two adults; dining for four; future dedicated desk; existing walls and openings retained. Room area from the supplied dimensions: **18 m²**. These are furniture-zoning studies, not a verified fit-out plan. Door/window positions, swings, sill heights, ceiling/beam heights and service points remain unknown. They need not prevent this concept comparison, but they prevent fixing the furniture positions or confirming clear routes. Jurisdiction is unconfirmed; no statutory compliance conclusion is made.

All furniture sizes below are **proposed test-fit allowances**, not measured facts, selected products or code minima. Sketches are not to scale; the next measured plan should be developed at 1:50.

| | A — Fitted dining edge | B — Movable furniture sequence |
|---|---|---|
| Spatial idea | A built-in banquette anchors one end; the living area remains the main open volume. | A freestanding dining group and sofa create two related zones, with room to rearrange them. |
| Dining for four | Proposed 1.4 m long banquette for two, with a 1.2 × 0.75 m table and two opposite chairs. Access to the inner seat needs testing. | Proposed 1.2 × 0.75 m table with two chairs on each long side. Four independently accessible seats, subject to the occupied-chair check. |
| Living | Proposed compact 2.0–2.1 × 0.9 m sofa along a long wall; small movable side tables. | Similar sofa, with small nesting tables that can move when the desk is introduced. |
| Future desk | Reserve a 1.2 × 0.6 m desk footprint at the opposite short end, plus chair space. Keep that zone free of permanent storage now. | Reserve the same desk footprint; a movable console can occupy it initially and be removed later. |
| Fixed versus flexible | Stronger spatial order and potential under-seat storage, but the dining location is hard to change. | Easier to reverse ends, shift the dining group or relocate the desk when the openings drawing arrives. |
| Main tradeoff | Banquette access and the fixed relationship to doors/windows. | Loose chairs need more operating space, and the composition needs restraint to avoid clutter. |

**Concept plans — orientation is arbitrary, not an assumed entrance or window location:**

```text
A: approximately 2.4 m dining + 3.6 m living/work reserve = 6 m
┌──────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┐
│ fitted bench         │ compact sofa       desk reserve│
│ table + 2 chairs     │                    at short end│ 3 m
│ route alongside      │ open living area + chair space │
└──────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┘

B: approximately 2.4 m dining + 3.6 m living/work reserve = 6 m
┌──────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┐
│ 2 chairs             │ compact sofa       console now │
│ freestanding table   │                    desk later  │ 3 m
│ 2 chairs + side route│ movable tables + chair space   │
└──────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┘
```

The line between zones is only a diagram marker, not a partition. Either arrangement can be mirrored or reversed to retain the actual openings.

**Plan checks:** test the room with all four dining seats occupied, then with dining and desk occupied together. Across the 3 m width, Option A could provisionally allocate 0.55 m bench depth + 0.75 m table + 0.70 m chair-use zone + 1.00 m passage. Option B could allocate 0.65 m chair-use zone + 0.75 m table + 0.65 m chair-use zone + 0.95 m passage. These sums show why narrow furniture matters; table/bench overlap, chair pull-out and actual human movement must be checked with the chosen furniture. The passage must connect the real doors without crossing occupied chairs. None of these dimensions establishes an accessible or statutory escape route.

For the future desk, initially reserve about 0.8 m behind its front edge for chair use, in addition to its 0.6 m depth. This makes a 1.4 m deep working band at the end of the living zone, leaving about 2.2 m of the proposed 3.6 m length for the sofa. That is a tight conceptual fit: do not add a deep cabinet opposite the desk or count the chair space as a through-route. In A the dining stays fixed when the desk arrives; in B the console leaves and the sofa/table positions can shift. Dining for four remains in both scenarios.

**Section and visual hierarchy:** retain the existing ceiling initially. Check a section through seating and the future desk once floor-to-ceiling height, beams and window sill/head heights are available:

```text
existing ceiling / beams — levels to be surveyed
──────────────────────────────────────────────
  seated view → across low furniture → focal wall/view
  sofa or bench              desk / seated user
──────────────────────────────────────────────
existing floor
```

For A, keep the bench back visually low enough to avoid screening an existing opening; for B, use low movable pieces so the room reads as one volume. Neither tall storage nor a lowered ceiling is needed to mark the zones. Make the living group the primary focus, with dining secondary and the future desk visually quiet. The actual focal wall or view depends on the openings; test both seated and standing sightlines before choosing it. A small sample grouping of a light neutral background, one timber tone and muted upholstery can test this hierarchy without committing to finishes.

**Provisional preference: B**, because the brief includes future change and the opening locations are still to come. A is worth retaining if the drawing reveals an uninterrupted wall for the bench and the users prefer integrated dining/storage. Select between them after a measured overlay checks door swings, window operation, chair movement, desk power access and any particular mobility needs. Approve that preferred concept before developing joinery or ordering furniture.
