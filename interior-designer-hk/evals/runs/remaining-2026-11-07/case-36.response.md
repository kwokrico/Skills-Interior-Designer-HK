# Handover and payment position

**No—the video does not establish a legal 30-day deadline or a general right to withhold all payment.** Key delivery alone does not establish completion, acceptance or waiver of defects. Your signed contract, agreed scope and actual usability must be checked before deciding what payment is due.

| Item | Assessment and immediate action |
|---|---|
| Bunk bed cannot be accessed | A functional failure, not merely a cosmetic snag. Keep the affected bed out of use until safe access is restored. Record photographs, measurements and the approved drawing; require the contractor to propose rectification and demonstrate safe access and operation at reinspection. Whether it prevents contractual completion depends on the agreed use and completion criteria. |
| Missing cabinet panel | Incomplete scope. It may be a minor outstanding item only if it is decorative and does not affect safety, stability, protection or use. Confirm its purpose before accepting that classification. Record part identification, dimensions, finish, supplier, confirmed installation date and reinspection. |
| Basic cleaning excludes windows | Record that exclusion against the agreed quotation. “Basic cleaning” does not automatically promise a move-in-ready clean or include windows. If the exclusion was not agreed, resolve the scope against the contract; if agreed, arrange window cleaning separately with an itemized scope and safe access arrangements. |

The closeout guidance treats the video's **30 days as a term to negotiate**, not an existing statutory entitlement. Do not wait 30 days to report an urgent defect or allow a contractual notice deadline to expire.

If your contract expressly adopts the unamended HKRG standard quotation:

- Clause 3 assesses completion by reasonable occupation and use, which makes the unusable bed material to the assessment.
- Clause 10 permits holding back the costs associated with snag rectification when paying the balance, with the remaining balance payable within one week after rectification. It does **not** grant an unrestricted right to hold every payment.
- Clause 12 contains a three-month repair provision after notice for qualifying defects within the agreed warranty period and subject to its conditions. That is a model contractual provision, not a universal deadline or permission to leave an unsafe item in use.

These provisions apply only if incorporated into your contract and subject to its amendments. The Consumer Council likewise recommends agreeing defect-rectification and final-payment arrangements expressly in the contract and jointly documenting outstanding work. [Consumer Council guidance](https://www.consumer.org.hk/en/press-release/530-renovation-complaint)

Prepare one written outstanding-work schedule now, with separate entries for the bed, panel and cleaning scope. For each, record the responsible party, agreed completion date, access arrangements, photographs and closure test. If you agree partial handover, identify exactly what is accepted, what remains excluded, and any restrictions on use. Record the payment milestone, amount disputed or retained, its contractual basis, and release condition separately; obtain both parties' written agreement. Do not describe all work as accepted simply because keys changed hands.

**Needed to confirm your actual entitlement:** the signed completion/payment/retention and defects clauses, quotation and cleaning exclusions, any handover document already signed, and the video link or transcript. If payment remains disputed, have the contract reviewed before imposing a blanket withholding position.
