# Early concept comparison — 6 m × 3 m living/dining room

**Brief:** two adults, dining for four, provision for a future desk; walls and openings retained. The stated rectangle is 18 m². Door/window locations and swings are pending, so these are furniture-zoning proposals, not verified fit-outs. No entrance, window, daylight direction or uninterrupted wall is assumed.

**Provisional preference: Option A for flexibility; Option B if you favour a larger open route and more compact dining.** Both can be mirrored or reversed once the openings are plotted.

| | A — Separate lounge, freestanding dining and work bay | B — Lounge with wall-side bench dining and work bay |
|---|---|---|
| Organisation | Divide the length into approximately 2.8 m lounge, 2.0 m dining and 1.2 m future work bay. | Use the same broad zoning, but move dining toward a long wall with a loose two-person bench and two chairs opposite. |
| Living | Propose a compact two-seat sofa along a long wall; small movable side tables keep the centre adaptable. | Similar sofa grouping, with the bench and dining table continuing along that side to create a calmer furniture line. |
| Dining for four | Test a 1,200 × 750 mm table, long dimension parallel to the 6 m room length, with two chairs on each long side. Avoid end chairs in this compact zone. | Test the same table with two bench places against one long wall and two chairs facing it. Keep the table movable so bench users can enter and leave. |
| Future desk | Reserve a 1,000–1,200 × 600 mm desk footprint along a long wall in the work bay, plus its occupied chair space. Keep this bay free of permanent storage. | Reserve the same separate work bay. The dining bench saves space across the room, but does not remove the need for desk-chair space. |
| Main advantage | All four dining seats are easier to approach; loose furniture can adapt to the eventual door/window plan. | Dining projects less into the room, leaving more width on its open side. |
| Main compromise | A centred dining table can interrupt through movement when chairs are occupied. | Bench access is less convenient; one person may need to move to let another out. Requires a suitable clear wall segment. |
| Best suited to | Frequent four-person meals and changing furniture needs. | Mostly two-person daily use, with four-person dining when needed. |

The furniture sizes above are proposed test-fit envelopes, not selected products or statutory minima. For example, a centred 750 mm-wide table leaves `(3,000 − 750) ÷ 2 = 1,125 mm` on each side **before chairs and seated people are allowed for**. That does not demonstrate a clear passage behind occupied chairs. Likewise, a 600 mm-deep desk leaves 2,400 mm across the room before the desk chair and any opposing furniture are counted.

Keep the future desk usable while four people dine: do not count the same chair space twice, and avoid relying on the dining table as the only future workstation. Use movable tables rather than a large central coffee table until the circulation route is known.

When the drawings arrive, overlay existing door swings, window operation and curtain space, then test all four occupied dining seats, the desk chair and the continuous route through the room together. Confirm that the 6 × 3 m dimensions are finished internal dimensions and note any projections or fixed services. This is enough to advance the concept now; final placement, circulation and any jurisdiction-specific compliance conclusions remain pending that information. The next plan can be developed at 1:50.
