Choose the **STC-rated partition system as the basis for confidential conversations**. Add absorptive panels if the room also needs echo control. The supplier’s claim is misleading: NRC and STC measure different things, so their numbers cannot be compared.

| Rating | What it tells you | Relevance to this room |
|---|---|---|
| **NRC — Noise Reduction Coefficient** | How well a surface absorbs sound, reducing reflections and reverberation within a room. Higher NRC generally means greater absorption under comparable test and mounting conditions. | Improves listening comfort and speech clarity inside; does not establish how well the enclosure stops speech escaping. |
| **STC — Sound Transmission Class** | A single-number rating of airborne sound insulation through a tested assembly. Higher STC generally means better isolation under comparable conditions. | Relevant to reducing speech transmission to adjacent spaces, but the partition’s laboratory rating alone does not guarantee confidentiality. |

These are separate performance measures: see [ASTM C423, absorption testing](https://store.astm.org/standards/c423) and [ASTM E413, sound-insulation ratings](https://store.astm.org/e0413-10.html). An NRC of 0.90 is not “90% soundproof,” and STC 50 is not a promise of 50 dB reduction at every frequency.

Specify and check the **whole room enclosure**: partition continuity to the structural soffit or an appropriately designed acoustic closure; doors with effective perimeter and threshold seals; glazing; wall junctions; sockets and service penetrations; and bypass paths through ceiling voids, raised floors and shared ventilation ducts. A gap or weak door can undermine a highly rated wall.

Ask the supplier for the test report for the **exact complete partition assembly**, including boards, studs, insulation, joints and installation details. For absorbers, request the tested thickness, mounting/air gap and frequency-dependent absorption data. Compare like-for-like reports, not headline numbers.

Before selecting a final STC target, establish the required confidentiality level, expected speaking levels, adjacent uses and background noise, plus the existing enclosure construction. The skill’s generic meeting-room benchmark of STC 45+ is only a starting point, not a confidentiality acceptance criterion. Agree a measurable room-level privacy requirement and verify the completed installation; [ASTM E2638](https://store.astm.org/e2638-24.html) assesses closed-room speech privacy using both enclosure insulation and background noise outside the room.
